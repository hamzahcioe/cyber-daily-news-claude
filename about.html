About and method

A daily news briefing on cybersecurity, major outages, technology and major international news, with analysis of the news. It is reporting only: no advice, and no tailoring to any organisation.

How it is made

An automated process collects stories from over a hundred public feeds each working day, groups reports of the same event, and researches each selected story from its primary source where one can be read: vendor advisories, maintainer announcements, status pages, regulator and court documents, company filings. A separate check compares the draft against the quotes the research was based on before anything is published. Briefs are published as static files and are never edited silently: when a story changes, a dated update is added.

Confidence labels

CONFIRMED The affected party or an authority has acknowledged it, or multiple reputable outlets have verified it.

VENDOR-ACKNOWLEDGED A vendor or company has said something, but details are thin.

UNCONFIRMED CLAIM A claim, rumour, state-media assertion or single-source report that has not been verified. Each item states the basis for its label.

Severity

Critical, High, Medium, Low or Awareness. It is a general assessment based on scale, exploitation status, ease of exploitation, vendor-assigned severity and the number of people or organisations affected. It is not tailored to any organisation. Non-security tech stories are marked Awareness unless there is a significant security or market consequence.

Outlet counts

Where a story shows "covered by N outlets", the count is the number of distinct sites among the sources cited for it. It is a rough signal of how widely a story is being reported, not a measure of accuracy.

Global scope and neutrality

Coverage deliberately spans regions, including China, Russia, Ukraine, the Middle East, South and East Asia, Africa and Latin America. Contested claims are attributed to whoever made them, state-run media is labelled as such, and where sides give conflicting accounts each account is reported. The same standard applies to every government, company and lab.

Sections

Top stories; analysis of how the day's stories connect; ongoing issues; cyber categories (outages, attacks and campaigns, breaches, leaks, ransomware and unconfirmed claims, supply chain, vulnerabilities, law enforcement and policy); Microsoft and UK views; patch watch; coming up; world and technology (major international news, economy and trade, geopolitics and tech, AI, energy and climate tech, physical and infrastructure attacks, state spying and surveillance, major tech news); and a short list of stories being watched.

Limits

The process can miss stories, particularly very recent social-media reports and sources that block automated reading. Some primary sources cannot be read automatically; where that happens the item says so and its label reflects the secondary reporting. Errors are corrected with a dated update.
